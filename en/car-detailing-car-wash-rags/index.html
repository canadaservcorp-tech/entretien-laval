Where a recycled rag beats a microfiber

Microfibers are expensive and get ruined quickly on dirty surfaces. For the messy jobs, a recycled rag does the work and you can toss it without regret:

  • Wheels, tires and wheel wells.
  • Engine bays, grease and oil.
  • Door jambs, sills and trunks.
  • Cleaning buckets, tools and the wash bay.

What about the paint?

For drying and polishing paint, most detailers prefer microfiber. If you use cotton, choose white T-shirt rags, with no color transfer.

Which product to choose

Colored terry ($32 per 25 lb bag) absorbs lots of water and grime for wheels and engines. Colored T-shirt rags suit interiors and plastics.

Keeping costs down

A detailer who uses recycled rags for wheels, engines and jambs replaces fewer microfibers. Keep one bin of colored rags for the dirty jobs and a separate bin of microfibers for paint, so they never mix.

Frequently asked questions

Can recycled rags scratch paint?

Any cloth can scratch if it drags dirt. On paint, use a clean, soft rag like white T-shirt, or a microfiber.

Can car wash rags be laundered?

Yes, if they aren't soaked with solvent. Wash them separately and skip fabric softener, which reduces absorbency.
