Soiled rags at work: storage, pickup and disposal

In a garage, shop or plant, soiled rags are a fire-safety and environmental issue. Here's a simple procedure to set up, and what to check with the authorities.

Three kinds of rags

  • Clean: in their closed bag or a lidded bin, away from chemicals.
  • Lightly soiled (water, dust, mild cleaners): can be washed and reused. See storing, washing and disposing of rags.
  • Soiled (oil, grease, solvents, fuel, paint, stain): follow the procedure below.

Storing soiled rags

  1. A metal container with a self-closing lid, made for oily rags, at every workstation where oils or solvents are used.
  2. Clearly labelled (“Soiled rags only”) so nothing else goes in.
  3. Away from heat sources: welding, heaters, compressors, direct sunlight.
  4. Emptied daily or at the end of each shift into a closed storage container, away from the building if possible.
  5. Never in a pile, never in an open trash can or a dryer. Rags soaked with linseed oil or stain can self-ignite: see oily rags and fire risk.

Pickup and disposal

Depending on what they absorbed, soiled rags may be considered residual hazardous materials under Québec's Regulation respecting hazardous materials. So they don't go in the regular trash. In practice, businesses:

  • use a hazardous waste pickup company, which often supplies the containers and handles transport;
  • or use an industrial shop towel rental and laundry service that takes back the dirty rags;
  • keep proof of pickup (slips, invoices) on file.

The exact rules depend on the products used, the quantities and your municipality. Check them with Québec's Ministry of the Environment, your municipality and your waste hauler.

Training the team

  • The products you use fall under WHMIS: their safety data sheets (SDS) explain how to store and dispose of soiled materials.
  • Post the procedure near the containers and show it to every new employee.
  • If in doubt about fire prevention, check with your municipality's fire department.

Checklist

  • One self-closing metal container per work area.
  • Containers labelled and emptied daily.
  • Clean rags kept apart from soiled rags.
  • A waste hauler or laundry service chosen, with proof of pickup kept.
  • SDSs available and the team trained.

Frequently asked questions

Can an oily rag go in the company trash?

No. Keep it in a closed metal container and have it collected by a specialized company or an industrial rag laundry service, under the rules that apply to you.

Are soiled rags hazardous waste?

It depends on what they absorbed and the quantities. Sometimes they are residual hazardous materials under Québec's Regulation respecting hazardous materials. Check with your waste hauler or the Ministry of the Environment.

Do oily rags need a special container?

A metal container with a self-closing lid, designed for oily rags, is the recommended practice. Empty it every day.
