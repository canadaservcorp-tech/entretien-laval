The right rag: white cotton T-shirt

White T-shirt rags are thin cotton jersey: they absorb water, leave little lint and transfer no dye. They're our best pick for glass and stainless steel. Recycled cotton isn't completely lint-free: see lint-free rags.

Windows and mirrors

  1. First remove dust and heavy dirt with a damp rag.
  2. Apply your usual glass cleaner, without soaking the surface.
  3. Wipe with a clean white rag, then finish with a second, dry rag: that's the step that removes streaks.
  4. Switch rags as soon as one gets damp: a wet rag just spreads dirt.

For large shop windows, a squeegee does most of the work; the dry rag handles edges and corners.

Stainless steel

  • Wipe with the grain (the fine lines in brushed metal), never in circles.
  • Use soapy water or a stainless steel cleaner, then dry with a dry white rag.
  • Avoid bleach and chlorine products on stainless steel, as well as abrasive pads: they can mark the surface.
  • Always follow the equipment and cleaner manufacturers' directions.

Common mistakes

  • Fabric softener: if you wash your rags, skip it. It leaves a film that streaks and reduces absorbency.
  • Coloured rags: with some cleaners, dyed fabric can bleed onto grout or pale surfaces.
  • One rag for everything: keep white rags just for glass, separate from kitchen or shop rags.

Frequently asked questions

Which rag cleans windows without streaks?

A thin white cotton rag, clean, with a dry one for the final wipe. Among our products: white T-shirt rags, $42 per 25 lb 25 lb bag before tax.

Should stainless steel be wiped in circles?

No. Wipe with the grain of the metal to avoid marks, then dry with a dry rag.

Does recycled cotton replace microfiber for glass?

For most glass and stainless steel, a clean white T-shirt rag does the job well. Microfiber is still useful for very delicate surfaces or screens.
