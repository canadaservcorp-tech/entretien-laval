Oily rags: the fire risk and how to dispose of them

A pile of rags soaked with linseed oil or stain can catch fire on its own. Here's how to prevent it.

Request a quoteinfo@entretienlaval.ca

Why a rag can catch fire on its own

Some oils, such as linseed oil, tung oil and many oil-based stains and varnishes, harden by reacting with oxygen in the air. That reaction gives off heat. In a pile or a ball of rags the heat can't escape, so the temperature climbs until it ignites, sometimes hours later. This is spontaneous combustion.

Rags soaked with gasoline, thinner or solvents don't self-ignite the same way, but their vapours are highly flammable near a flame or spark.

What to do

  1. Never leave rags in a pile, in an open trash can or in a corner of the shop.
  2. Dry them flat, outdoors, away from buildings, children and pets, before throwing them out.
  3. Or put them in a metal container with a tight lid, filled with water, until you can get rid of them.
  4. At work, use a self-closing metal oily-waste can and empty it every day.
  5. Don't put oil-soaked rags in a dryer, even after washing: dryer fires start this way.

Where to dispose of them in Québec

For households, rags soaked with oil or solvent are household hazardous waste: take them to your city's eco-centre in a closed container. Businesses must dispose of them under their hazardous materials rules, often through a specialized provider.

If in doubt, check with your municipality's fire department.

Choosing the right rag

For stain and varnish, white T-shirt rags won't bleed. For oil and grease in the garage, colored terry absorbs the most.

Frequently asked questions

How long before a rag catches fire?

Anywhere from a few hours to more than a day, depending on the oil, the amount and the temperature. That's why a pile should never be left unattended.

Can a gasoline rag ignite on its own?

Not the same way as linseed oil, but its vapours ignite easily. Dry it outdoors, away from any ignition source.
